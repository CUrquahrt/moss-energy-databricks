The idea

The manufacturing playbook, applied to a solar build

The disciplines that run a high-mix electronics line map directly onto an EPC portfolio. What usually fails is the data underneath: schedule, procurement, field production, and quality live in separate systems, so variance shows up in the weekly report instead of on the day it happens.

On the line
Takt time → install rate

Piles, tracker rows, and modules per crew-day against plan, for every block on every site.

On the line
SPC → crew control charts

Catch a crew or a pile rig drifting out of control in days, not at the monthly schedule update.

On the line
MRP → module & tracker flow

Tie POs, port and rail ETAs, and laydown inventory to the install sequence that consumes them.

On the line
First-pass yield → commissioning

Punch-list, IV-curve, and torque QA data linked back to the crew and block that produced it.

What it costs to wait

Schedule variance is the most expensive number in the portfolio

Every slipped COD day carries liquidated damages, extended general conditions, and deferred revenue for the owner. The earlier variance is visible, the cheaper it is to fix. Our illustrative model for Moss puts the cost of delaying this foundation at roughly $1.3M per quarter, and schedule compression is the largest lever.

Schedule

P6 says on track, the field says otherwise

Percent-complete is reported weekly and by hand, so slip compounds for weeks before anyone acts on it.

Supply chain

Module and tracker timing drives the critical path

Tariff, FEOC, and port exposure changes delivery dates, but those changes never reach the install sequence automatically.

Field production

Ground conditions show up as lost crew-days

Pile refusal, caliche, and weather appear as productivity loss with no root cause attached.

The ask

A 60-minute working session, then a 4–6 week POC on one live site

We'd bring the Databricks solar/EPC team and work through which project, which sources, and what success looks like to you. The POC ends with this Command Center running on your real data.

WEEKS 1–2

Connect & model

Ingest P6, procurement, and field production for one active site into the existing workspace. Agree the gold metric definitions.

WEEKS 3–4

Forecast & control

Train the COD-slip and driver model on site history and turn on crew SPC alerts. Launch Genie for the project team.

WEEKS 5–6

Prove the value

Back-test the forecast against actuals, run what-if scenarios with the PM, and size the rollout across the Energy portfolio.

Success test: did the model flag slip earlier than the weekly schedule update? If it did, roll it out. If it didn't, you've lost six weeks and kept the data foundation.
